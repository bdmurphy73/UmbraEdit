### Scene 3.1: Story Comments

@tag: Scene.StoryComments
@pov: Narrator

## Story Comments

Story comments start with `%Story.` followed by a keyword of your choosing. These comments are used to track story metadata and are never included in the word count. You can select to include or exclude them from your manuscript when exporting.

%Story.Resolution: This scene resolves the mystery of the missing watch.
%Story.Pacing: The pacing in this scene is deliberate to build tension.
%Story.CharacterArc: Jane learns to trust her instincts in this scene.

% The part after the dot in your story comment is a keyword of your choosing. You can define your own keywords for tracking whatever matters to your story.

## Example from the Story

Alice was beginning to get very tired of sitting by her sister on the bank, and of having nothing to do: once or twice she had peeped into the book her sister was reading, but it had no pictures or conversations in it, “and what is the use of a book,” thought Alice “without pictures or conversations?”

%Story.Setting: The scene takes place on a riverbank on a hot summer day.

So she was considering in her own mind whether the pleasure of making a daisy-chain would be worth the trouble of getting up and picking the daisies, when suddenly a White Rabbit with pink eyes ran close by her.

%Story.PlotPoint: The White Rabbit's appearance initiates the adventure.

## Using Story Comments

Story comments are useful for:
- Tracking which scenes resolve which plot points
- Marking scenes that need revision
- Noting character development moments
- Tracking pacing and tension
- Any other story metadata you want to track
